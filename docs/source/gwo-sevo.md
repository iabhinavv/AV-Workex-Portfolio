This document reconstructs the work visible in the available project conversations, planning archive, commercial presentation, working files, and delivery records. It covers an affiliate network and its related managed creator marketing service.

The record includes business development, merchant research, sales enablement, creator scouting, portfolio development, campaign shortlisting, bulk onboarding preparation, and performance reporting. It also includes agency tooling and a partnership event that were explored as possible extensions.

Names, contact details, account identifiers, domains, creator handles, client references, actual commercial results, private budgets, and identifying file locations have been omitted. Examples and templates below use generic fields. Source labels refer to types of evidence rather than identifiable documents.

Three labels distinguish history from the procedure proposed for future use:

- **Recorded work:** A request, source file, implementation, or delivery is visible in the available record. A completed deliverable does not establish a resulting sale or successful campaign.
- **Explored:** A model, tool, activity, or capability was discussed or proposed; execution is not established.
- **Replication standard:** A recommended procedure or control added here to make the work repeatable. It is not a claim that every control existed historically.

## 3. Business model to reproduce

The model brings together four participants:

1. **Advertiser:** Supplies the product or service, campaign objective, approved offer, and payment terms.
2. **Publisher or creator:** Reaches a relevant audience through approved promotional activity.
3. **Customer:** Visits, enquires, purchases, or completes the agreed action.
4. **Network:** Coordinates relationships, campaign setup, tracking requirements, reporting, validation, and settlement.

Two related services emerged in the work:

| Service | What the buyer receives | Commercial basis to define |
|---|---|---|
| Performance affiliate campaign | Distribution through publishers or creators, with attributable activity | Sale, qualified lead, click, impression, or another defined action |
| Managed creator campaign | Brief interpretation, creator selection, commercial coordination, campaign delivery, and reporting | Agreed deliverables, management scope, and any approved performance component |

The commercial presentation described several payment models. That does not mean all were deployed. A pitch using performance payment should be used only when the specific campaign's event definition, attribution method, exclusions, and settlement terms support it. Do not carry a performance-only promise into a fixed-fee creator proposal by default.

**Replication standard:** Prove one narrow offer before expanding. Define the audience, the business problem, the delivery method, the payable event or deliverable, and the evidence needed to validate it.

## 6. SOP: Define the offer and delivery boundary

**Basis:** Recorded commercial model and agency workflow; approval controls below are replication standards.

**Input:** Target buyer, available distribution, operational capacity, and campaign objective.  
**Owner:** Business development, with operations and reporting.  
**Output:** One-page offer and campaign definition.

1. Choose a buyer segment and a problem that can be discussed concretely: attributable sales, qualified enquiries, creator discovery, or managed campaign execution.
2. Decide whether the offer is performance affiliate distribution, a managed creator service, or a clearly priced combination.
3. Define the desired event or deliverable. Specify what qualifies, what does not, and who validates it.
4. Separate responsibilities: advertiser approvals, publisher activity, network coordination, tracking ownership, and payment handling.
5. List what can be demonstrated today and what still needs to be built or verified.
6. Prepare the smallest pilot that can establish delivery quality and commercial value.
7. State the next step: a discovery meeting, a shortlist, an implementation review, or an approved pilot.

**Acceptance:** A colleague can explain the offer, payment trigger, delivery responsibilities, and evidence required without relying on unsupported claims.

## 7. SOP: Build and maintain the merchant pipeline

**Basis:** Recorded merchant-list enrichment and outreach-status handling. Prioritization and stage gates are replication standards.

**Input:** Existing prospect register, target sectors, and suitable business sources.  
**Owner:** Business development.  
**Output:** Qualified prospect pipeline with assigned next actions.

1. Preserve the original list before enrichment. Retain existing status notes and stable record IDs.
2. Define selection criteria: relevant audience, suitable offer, ability to fulfill demand, measurable objective, and a viable commercial conversation.
3. Group prospects by broad sector and likely use case. Avoid using one identical pitch for every business.
4. Research published business contact channels and relevant decision-making functions.
5. Distinguish a verified decision-maker route from a general company contact. Leave unverified details blank.
6. Respect existing outreach status. The historical enrichment work deliberately preserved already-marked rows rather than treating the whole file as new leads.
7. Assign an owner, the next action, and a follow-up date to every active opportunity.
8. Record the source and verification date privately. Keep personal contact data out of shared operating documents.

Suggested pipeline:

`Researched → Ready for outreach → Contacted → Discovery → Qualified → Proposal → Commercial review → Pilot approved → Active → Renew / Close`

Use separate outcomes for declined, deferred, and unresponsive prospects. A delivered message is not a qualified opportunity; a warm meeting is not a signed client.

**Acceptance:** Active opportunities have a clear status, owner, verified business route, and next step. Existing records remain traceable.

## 8. SOP: Run discovery and follow up

**Basis:** Recorded review of follow-up after an in-person prospect discussion. The repeatable call structure is a replication standard.

**Input:** Prospect context and current stage.  
**Owner:** Business development.  
**Output:** Completed campaign brief and an agreed next action.

### Discovery sequence

1. Establish the immediate objective: sales, qualified leads, awareness, content, or another specific outcome.
2. Understand the product, target audience, geography, seasonality, and any fulfillment constraints.
3. Ask what has been tried, what is working, and what the prospect wants to improve.
4. Discuss budget, acquisition costs, and commercial constraints at an appropriate point in the conversation. Avoid making a large sensitive-data request the first follow-up action.
5. Confirm who approves the proposal, who owns campaign delivery, and who can support tracking or reporting.
6. Define the action or deliverable that would make a pilot worthwhile.
7. Agree on the next meeting or document, its owner, and its due date.

### Follow-up structure

Use the actual conversation as the starting point. Briefly restate the buyer's need, connect the relevant service to that need, request only the missing information needed for the next step, and propose one specific action.

Attach only relevant material. A selected-creator portfolio may be more useful than a general presentation once the brief is understood.

**Acceptance:** Both sides understand the next step. The private brief records the objective, audience, timing, decision process, information gaps, and working commercial assumptions.

## 9. SOP: Enable the sales team and test messaging

**Basis:** Recorded sales-training request, outreach review, and repeated short-video script development. Testing procedures are replication standards; ad performance is not established by script reviews.

**Input:** Validated offer, buyer objections, and approved proof.  
**Owner:** Business development.  
**Output:** Pitch structure, objection guide, and message experiment log.

Teach the team a consistent sequence:

1. **Audience and problem:** Identify who the offer serves and the issue being solved.
2. **Mechanism:** Explain how selection, coordination, distribution, tracking, or reporting helps.
3. **Evidence:** Show a relevant process, authorized example, or verified capability.
4. **Scope:** State what is included and what depends on the brief or approval.
5. **Next step:** Ask for one action with a clear purpose.

For short-form advertising, the historical work explored three useful themes: rising acquisition costs, uncertainty about creator fit, and the time required to find and manage creators. These are angles to test, not proven winning messages.

Reusable pitch outline:

> We help [buyer segment] address [specific problem] through [the relevant affiliate or creator service]. We start by understanding your objective, then prepare [the next concrete deliverable]. We agree on scope, measurement, and commercial terms before launch. The next step is [one specific meeting or action].

Adapt the pitch to the buyer's stated need. Explain creator selection for a buyer struggling to find suitable talent; explain event validation and attribution for a buyer considering performance distribution. Do not lead with every service the network might eventually offer.

Review copy for audience clarity, hook, problem relevance, service explanation, proof, and call to action. Remove unsupported claims about network size, guaranteed returns, exceptional growth, market-first status, or delivery speed.

Test one variable at a time: hook, service explanation, call to action, or visual treatment. Keep the rest as consistent as practical. Record audience, spend, exposure, dates, and the resulting qualified action. Use qualified meetings and pilot opportunities alongside viewing metrics. Copy-review scores are editorial feedback, not a forecast of revenue.

**Acceptance:** The team can explain the service consistently, respond to common objections, and identify which claims require evidence before publication.

## 10. SOP: Build a reliable creator roster

**Basis:** Recorded scouting, category cleanup, data matching, and roster transfer. Verification controls below are replication standards.

**Input:** Supplied roster, public-profile evidence, and creator-provided information.  
**Owner:** Creator partnerships.  
**Output:** Master roster with verification status and an exception list.

1. Preserve the source and create stable internal creator IDs.
2. Count populated creator records, excluding headers, blank rows, and examples. A sheet's final row number is not its creator count.
3. Separate creator identity from platform accounts. One creator may have several accounts; one account is not another creator.
4. Normalize category labels into a small useful vocabulary. Keep the original category and the mapping privately so ambiguous changes can be reviewed.
5. Normalize platform labels, handles, and profile URLs. A constructed URL is a candidate link until verified.
6. Check that each screenshot, account, and creator record refers to the same person. Do not use file order as identity evidence.
7. Keep missing emails, phone numbers, images, and metrics explicitly missing. Do not fabricate contact routes or account data.
8. Record the metric's platform, meaning, source, and capture date.
9. Separate scouted, contacted, verified, contracted, and available status. A database entry does not establish an active partnership.
10. Maintain a queue for uncertain matches, unavailable profiles, stale metrics, and missing information.

### Essential metric controls

- A creator-level follower total is not automatically a separate verified follower count for every platform.
- Cross-platform followers are not deduplicated people unless a valid deduplication method exists.
- Highest observed views describe an exceptional post, not typical expected delivery.
- Unavailable likes, views, or followers should remain unavailable rather than display as zero.
- Tier labels need documented boundaries and must use the same rules in the roster and portfolio.

An earlier import adaptation copied a supplied creator-level total into platform audience fields because separate counts were unavailable. Preserve that limitation when interpreting those files. For replication, obtain platform-specific figures or use the receiving system's supported missing-value method. If the importer requires an unsupported number, resolve the schema issue rather than presenting an estimate as verified data.

**Acceptance:** Records have stable IDs, explicit account relationships, traceable category mappings, dated evidence, and a visible exception status.

## 11. SOP: Match creators to a campaign

**Basis:** Recorded specialist-first shortlisting, educational-format backups, and confidence notes. Final booking controls are replication standards.

**Input:** Approved brief and master roster.  
**Owner:** Creator partnerships with business development.  
**Output:** Ranked shortlist, supporting rationale, backups, and questions to resolve.

1. Translate the brief into selection criteria before selecting names.
2. Review direct subject specialists first.
3. Consider adjacent creators whose educational or demonstration format can communicate the product clearly.
4. Check actual recent content, audience relevance, communication style, and potentially unsuitable claims or associations.
5. Separate evidence from inference. A category in a spreadsheet is a lead for research, not proof of current content fit.
6. Label recommendations consistently: direct fit, adjacent fit, lower fit, or needs review.
7. Request current platform analytics, availability, pricing, and relevant work samples before booking.
8. Prepare backups and document what would change a recommendation.

| Selection question | Evidence needed |
|---|---|
| Can the creator explain the topic? | Relevant recent posts or a suitable demonstration |
| Does the audience match the brief? | Current audience information with a clear source |
| Are the numbers credible and usable? | Dated, platform-specific metrics and context |
| Is the association appropriate? | Content review and campaign-specific suitability checks |
| Can the creator deliver? | Confirmed availability, scope, commercial terms, and approvals |

**Acceptance:** Every shortlisted creator has a reason for inclusion, an evidence status, and explicit unresolved checks. Shortlisted does not mean approved or booked.

## 12. SOP: Build portfolio assets for the sales stage

**Basis:** Recorded directory, prospect preview, campaign shortlist, responsive refinements, and standalone-file packaging. Data-access controls below are replication standards.

**Input:** Approved roster subset, verified assets, and intended audience.  
**Owner:** Business development with data and development.  
**Output:** Reviewed portfolio and appropriate delivery package.

The build developed three related views:

| View | Purpose | Information approach |
|---|---|---|
| Full directory | Help an authorized reviewer compare a broad creator pool | Relevant categories, platforms, tiers, and individual profile detail |
| Prospect preview | Demonstrate breadth and invite a commercial conversation | Limited selection with a clear enquiry action |
| Campaign shortlist | Support a specific selection decision | Only relevant creators and approved presentation information |

### Build procedure

1. Decide who may receive the asset and what information that audience needs.
2. Use category, platform, and tier filters consistently across related pages. The recorded refinements removed free-text search and numeric follower-range controls to simplify the experience.
3. Make each creator card open a clear detail view. Keep layouts and labels consistent.
4. Preserve screenshot proportions. Enlarging or stretching a low-resolution image does not create new detail.
5. Use genuine profile evidence where available. Label missing-image placeholders clearly and omit unsupported metrics.
6. Test the complete path from filters to detail view to enquiry on phone and desktop layouts.
7. For a standalone portfolio, embed only authorized content and verify that the saved file opens without a separate asset folder.
8. For development handoff, include the required pages, assets, mapping manifest, and current integration notes.
9. Remove internal suitability notes and identifying account details when the intended audience should not receive them.

### Privacy lesson from the build

The historical preview used hidden handles, masked images, and a faded section to encourage enquiries. Those are presentation choices, not access controls. Text, URLs, metadata, embedded data, or recognizable images may remain recoverable from a file even when hidden visually.

**Replication standard:** If information must remain private, exclude it from the delivered file or enforce authorized access on the server. Do not place a complete private roster inside a public preview and rely on a visual lock.

**Acceptance:** The artifact works in its intended delivery form and contains only information suitable for its recipient. Interface changes do not expose restricted data.

## 13. SOP: Collect and manage profile evidence

**Basis:** Recorded image matching, platform screenshots, capture manifests, and unavailable-profile lists.

**Input:** Creator/platform inventory and permitted evidence sources.  
**Owner:** Creator partnerships with data support.  
**Output:** Matched evidence assets, a manifest, and a missing-evidence register.

1. Define the asset needed: portrait, complete platform profile, post example, or private analytics evidence. These serve different purposes.
2. Match the account before collecting an image.
3. Prefer original-quality assets where available. Record capture date and source privately.
4. Use an internal ID and platform code for shared filenames. Keep the identity mapping in the private register.
5. Track creator coverage separately from platform coverage. Evidence for one account does not prove that all of a creator's accounts have been covered.
6. Record blocked, inaccessible, unsupported, mismatched, and missing results separately.
7. Inspect the captured image itself. Correct dimensions do not prove that the page loaded, the content is readable, or the intended account is visible.
8. Avoid depending on external embeds in a file intended to work independently; the recorded build encountered access-denied pages and unreliable live embeds.
9. Keep illustrative mockups distinct from actual platform evidence.

**Acceptance:** Every included asset has a reliable record match. Missing coverage is explicit, and no portrait or mockup is presented as a genuine analytics screenshot.

## 14. SOP: Prepare a bulk onboarding file

**Basis:** Recorded template migrations, platform-field restructuring, nested-array repairs, and transfer audits. Controlled import and rollback procedures are replication standards.

**Input:** Master roster, current receiving template, and a known valid example.  
**Owner:** Data and development.  
**Output:** Validated import package, transfer summary, and exception file.

1. Duplicate the supplied template and preserve its tabs, column order, headers, and required fields.
2. Identify the receiving system's actual schema and linking keys. Use a known valid record and validator output to resolve ambiguity.
3. Build a source-to-target field map before moving the full dataset.
4. Replace dummy records. Count actual transferred records and account entries separately.
5. Preserve all supported platform accounts. Do not silently reduce a multi-platform creator to one account for convenience.
6. Match each target field's exact data type: text, number, list, nested list, or platform-keyed object. Similar-looking text is not necessarily valid structured data.
7. Validate structured cells as data, including quoting and required keys. Do not keep changing formats without identifying which requirement failed.
8. Keep unsupported or unknown values in the exception register. If a required linking field is missing, resolve it or obtain a supported alternative before import.
9. Check that every referenced image filename exists and matches the intended creator.
10. Test a small representative batch before the full import. Include records with multiple accounts, missing optional data, and unusual categories.
11. Back up the receiving data, inspect imported results, and reconcile accepted, rejected, and duplicate records.
12. Repair rejected records and retry only those records. Do not repeatedly submit the complete roster and create duplicates.

### Required transfer summary

| Check | Expected result |
|---|---|
| Source creator count | Reconciles with transferred creators plus documented exclusions |
| Account count | All supported source accounts are transferred or recorded as exceptions |
| Required linking keys | Present, valid, or handled by an approved alternative |
| Structured fields | Match the current schema and parse successfully |
| Image references | Existing file, correct match, or supported missing value |
| Metrics | Correct scope and source; no silent multiplication across platforms |
| Duplicate checks | Stable IDs and account identifiers checked before retry |
| Receiving-system review | Sample records render and behave correctly after import |

The available record includes workbook preparation and correction after reported partial uploads. File validation alone does not establish that the full final roster was successfully imported into production.

**Acceptance:** The receiving system accepts the representative batch, the reconciled results are reviewed, and a rollback route exists before the complete import.

## 15. SOP: Move an approved brief into campaign delivery

**Basis:** Commercial presentation and archived agency lifecycle. The detailed launch gate is a replication standard; a complete production execution system is not established by those sources.

**Input:** Qualified brief, selected commercial model, and proposed distribution.  
**Owner:** Campaign operations after business development handoff.  
**Output:** Approved campaign plan and auditable launch decision.

1. Confirm the objective, target audience, approved claims, budget responsibilities, timing, and deliverables.
2. Prepare a media or creator plan showing selections, rationale, formats, schedule, and costs.
3. Obtain client approval and confirm creator or publisher availability and terms.
4. Define the payable event, attribution window, duplicate rules, invalid activity, reversals, validation owner, and dispute process for performance work.
5. Assign campaign and partner identifiers in the private operating system.
6. Select and test the appropriate tracking method. The historical presentation described tracking links, deep links, pixels, server notifications, and integrations; those descriptions are capability claims, not proof that every method was implemented.
7. Test the journey from promotional link or content through the destination, event capture, reporting, and validation.
8. Confirm content approval, reporting access, contacts for exceptions, and payment responsibility.
9. Launch only after commercial, content, tracking, and operational readiness are accepted.
10. Monitor delivery, reconcile results, close exceptions, and obtain approval for settlement.

**Acceptance:** Every participant understands what is being delivered, what is measured, what is payable, and who can approve changes. Failed test events or missing approvals block launch.

## 16. SOP: Produce recurring performance reports

**Basis:** Recorded workbook-to-dashboard implementation and subsequent reporting refinements.

**Input:** Same-format channel workbook containing dated sales, commission, and channel-cost inputs.  
**Owner:** Reporting, with finance review for settlement decisions.  
**Output:** Reconciled dashboard, period export, and action notes.

### Reporting build that was completed

The reporting tool converted recurring spreadsheets into an interactive browser dashboard. It supported channel and date selection, daily/weekly/monthly views, a local/reporting-currency toggle, a combined line chart for sales, commission, cost, and the displayed profit metric, exports, and a saved standalone dashboard.

Later refinements simplified the header, removed a repetitive raw-data section, kept filters aligned across outputs, and changed the combined chart from bars to a line trend. Source amounts were retained in the base currency while displayed values could be converted and rounded.

### Repeatable reporting procedure

1. Preserve the source workbook. Confirm the expected sheets, date structure, labels, and numeric input fields.
2. Treat blank cells as missing and numeric zero as a known zero.
3. Import the workbook and review validation warnings before accepting the replacement report.
4. Recompute totals from the dated inputs. Do not rely blindly on spreadsheet summary or profit formulas.
5. Reconcile representative dates and channel totals against the source.
6. Review daily, weekly, and monthly results. A partial selected period should remain visibly partial.
7. Keep the same channel, date, period, and currency selection across charts, totals, and exports.
8. Keep missing values visible. Do not draw a continuous result through missing data or show incomplete profit as a complete total.
9. Save the reviewed snapshot when a durable copy is needed. A temporary import is not automatically a permanent update.
10. Record the business decision: investigate a discrepancy, reduce an inefficient channel, collect missing inputs, or run a further test.

### Metric definitions and limits

| Metric | Definition | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | Attributed sales value from the selected source records | Merchant sales value; not automatically network revenue |
| Commission | Commission recorded for the selected activity | Confirm approval and reversal status before settlement |
| Channel cost | Recorded media or messaging cost | Includes only costs actually supplied |
| Campaign contribution | Commission minus recorded channel cost | The historical dashboard displayed this as “Profit” |
| Commission rate | Eligible commission divided by corresponding sales | Use matching records and valid denominators |
| Sales-to-media ratio | Corresponding sales divided by channel cost | Not a network-profit measure |
| Contribution ROI | Corresponding contribution divided by channel cost | Not full-company ROI unless all relevant costs are included |

Campaign contribution is not full company net profit. Creator payouts, salaries, tools, taxes, and other expenses may be absent from that report. Check whether commission is gross or already net of partner payouts before subtracting those payouts elsewhere.

For ratios, use sums over matching complete records rather than averaging daily percentages. Label incomplete coverage. A zero or missing denominator produces an unavailable ratio, not an invented value.

For currency conversion, use a documented approved rate and direction. For example, if the rate is base-currency units per reporting-currency unit, divide the base amount by that rate. Preserve calculation precision and round only for display unless the export specification says otherwise. This document intentionally omits the historical rate.

### Verification checklist

- Missing inputs and true zero remain distinct.
- Weekly boundaries, monthly grouping, and partial periods behave consistently.
- New date columns are included without depending on a stale total range.
- Invalid dates, duplicate dates, unsupported formulas, and nonnumeric inputs are handled explicitly.
- Negative contribution and empty selections are displayed correctly.
- Failed imports do not erase the previous valid report.
- Changing a filter updates charts, totals, and exported results together.
- A saved standalone report reopens with the intended data and settings.

Automated checks and later browser checks are recorded for the dashboard. That is historical validation, not a new audit of its present deployment.

**Acceptance:** The report reconciles to valid source inputs, exposes material gaps, uses clear definitions, and reaches only its intended recipients. Saved dashboards can embed confidential data; a local file or obscured URL is not access protection.

## 17. SOP: Select an agency operating stack

**Basis:** Explored in the supplied planning ZIP. No final platform commitment is established.

The archive described a managed lifecycle: receive an advertiser brief, develop and share a media plan, and build a vetted local creator roster. It highlighted two practical operating needs: a familiar messaging channel for creator outreach and mobile-wallet support for local payments.

Three tool approaches were explored:

| Approach | Why it was considered | What to verify before choosing |
|---|---|---|
| Global influencer platform | Established discovery and campaign features | Actual target-market creator coverage and amount of manual roster entry |
| Regional creator platform | Potentially closer local coverage and campaign workflow | Coverage quality, export options, payment fit, and operating reliability |
| Flexible database plus forms | Lower starting complexity and direct control of a manually built roster | Permissions, data quality, workflow ownership, and growing administrative effort |

**Replication standard:** Compare tools using the same small anonymized test brief and sample records. Run intake, creator matching, media planning, approvals, reporting, and export. Choose the smallest stack that supports the real workflow. Upgrade when measured workload or a specific missing capability justifies it.

**Acceptance:** The chosen stack supports the target market and operating process, exports usable data, and has a named owner. A tool's feature list is not proof that it fits the local roster.

### Related expansion explored: sector-specific lead generation

A related feasibility exercise considered a focused financial-services lead-generation offer using existing messaging and digital acquisition capabilities. A retainer plus payment for qualified leads was explored. This was an adjacent business concept, not evidence of a launched network service or paying institutional clients.

For replication, agree on the qualification definition, permitted data collection and transfer, lead acceptance, tracking, and commercial terms before scaling acquisition. Validate a paid pilot before extrapolating to multiple institutions. Include the economic cost of shared staff and acquisition resources even when the immediate project cash budget does not pay those costs directly. Keep forecasts separate from realized results.

## 18. SOP: Evaluate a creator–brand partnership event

**Basis:** Explored event plan. Venue booking, sponsor commitments, event delivery, and event revenue are not established.

The concept used a curated gathering of advertisers, creators, and affiliates to create commercial introductions. Potential revenue sources included sponsorship, category packages, activation spaces, screen placements, and business-delegate access.

**Replication standard:**

1. Define the business outcome: qualified introductions, campaign briefs, partner recruitment, or pilot conversations.
2. Specify attendee selection criteria before setting capacity.
3. Turn sponsorship inventory into concrete deliverables, including placement, access, exclusivity, and reporting.
4. Collect comparable written venue and supplier quotes. Include production, hospitality, staffing, contingency, and cancellation exposure.
5. Build a cash plan using confirmed commitments and collections. Set a funding gate before making binding supplier commitments.
6. Track category exclusivity so the same exclusive right is not sold twice.
7. Design introductions and lead capture with clear permission for follow-up.
8. Assign owners for sponsor delivery, guest management, content, and commercial follow-up.
9. Measure qualified meetings, approved follow-ups, proposals, and resulting pilots after the event.

**Acceptance:** The event has a defined commercial purpose, funded delivery plan, agreed sponsor obligations, and follow-up ownership. Attendance or projected sponsorship does not equal realized revenue.

## 20. Operating cadence and replication sequence

This cadence is a replication standard rather than a record of the historical team's meeting schedule.

| Frequency | Review |
|---|---|
| During active delivery | Campaign exceptions, approvals, creator availability, and urgent tracking or payment issues |
| Weekly | Pipeline next steps, shortlist evidence, import exceptions, channel results, and experiment decisions |
| Monthly | Collections and payouts, cost coverage, roster quality, tool workload, and renewal opportunities |
| Before every release or share | Recipient scope, identifiers, embedded data, image content, and current handoff notes |

Repeat the build in this order:

1. Define one clear offer and its delivery boundary.
2. Prepare the pitch and an owned merchant pipeline.
3. Build and verify the creator roster.
4. Create the prospect preview and authorized review portfolio.
5. Run discovery and prepare a campaign-specific shortlist.
6. Validate the onboarding schema and import a small representative batch.
7. Approve a pilot with testable delivery and tracking requirements.
8. Launch, reconcile, report, and settle the approved activity.
9. Use evidence from the pilot to improve the offer and sales process.
10. Consider more complex tooling, new service lines, or events only when the core process supports them.

For each stage, retain its output and acceptance decision before expanding the next stage. The transferable method is the connection between commercial discovery, verified supply, usable sales assets, operational data, and decision-focused reporting.

## 21. Source coverage and publication boundary

| Evidence group | What it supports | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Archived planning overview and index | Agency lifecycle, local workflow considerations, and tooling options | Full historical transcripts or a final platform selection |
| Commercial presentation | Intended network model, campaign types, onboarding flow, and tracking proposition | Independent verification of promotional results or every advertised capability |
| Business development conversations | Leadership context, sales-enablement request, merchant enrichment, and follow-up review | Signed contracts or attributed revenue |
| Creator scouting and roster work | Campaign matching, data cleanup, evidence limitations, and shortlist development | Current audience figures or booking approval |
| Portfolio development records and files | Directory, preview, selected-creator assets, and iterative presentation changes | Production access control or a complete backend build |
| Import files, corrections, and transfer audits | Data mapping, schema adjustments, packaging, and reported partial-upload issues | Successful final production import of all records |
| Reporting source and delivery records | Reusable dashboard, calculation handling, interface refinements, and historical checks | Full company profitability or a new audit of the live system |
| Event planning conversation | Partnership-event concept and proposed operating model | An executed event, collected sponsorship, or realized event returns |
| Related lead-generation feasibility work | Sector focus, hybrid pricing exploration, pilot sequencing, and shared-resource costing | A launched network service, institutional contracts, or realized profitability |

The source references are deliberately generalized so this document can be shared without exposing identities or private locations. It contains no client case-study claims, live roster entries, contact details, actual campaign results, or private commercial amounts.

Before adapting this document for a new organization, add only approved information, retain the distinction between recorded work and recommendations, and update the procedure when real delivery evidence changes it.
