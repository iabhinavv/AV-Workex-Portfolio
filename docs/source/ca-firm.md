# Audit, accounting and taxation

Use one clear file trail from the client’s records to the final accounts, return or audit report. Keep the work easy to review: what was checked, what differed, how it was resolved and who approved it.

## Start the engagement

1. Confirm the entity, period, service, deadlines, reporting framework and responsible partner. Complete the engagement letter, appointment and independence checks where required.
2. Request the trial balance, ledgers, bank statements, previous accounts, returns and supporting schedules. Track missing items with an owner and follow-up date.
3. Assign a preparer and reviewer. Agree the scope, sampling approach and escalation route before fieldwork starts.

**Output:** An agreed scope, records checklist and work allocation.

## Finalise the books

1. Tie opening balances to the previous signed accounts. Reconcile bank accounts, receivables, payables, inventory and statutory balances.
2. Check revenue and expense cut-off, fixed assets, depreciation, loans, interest, payroll and provisions. Record each adjustment with its reason and approval.
3. Map the adjusted trial balance into the balance sheet, profit and loss account, cash flow statement where required, and supporting notes.
4. Cross-check totals, comparatives and disclosures. Clear review queries before finalisation.

**Review point:** Every material balance should lead back to a ledger and a supporting schedule. Investigate differences; never use a balancing entry to hide them.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Book-finalisation checklist</summary>

- Bank reconciliation: outstanding items, charges, stale entries and subsequent clearance.
- Receivables and payables: ageing, confirmations where needed, recoverability and unrecorded liabilities.
- Inventory and assets: quantities, valuation, additions, disposals and depreciation.
- Borrowings and dues: agreements, interest, repayments and unpaid statutory liabilities.
- Statements: adjusted trial balance, note references, related parties, contingencies and comparative figures.

</details>

## Prepare tax workings and returns

Confirm the legislation, rates, forms, filing dates and authorisation requirements for the relevant period before preparing a return.

| Workstream | Main reconciliation | Completion evidence |
|---|---|---|
| TDS | Expense and payroll ledgers to deductions, challans and statements | Reviewed workings, challans and filing acknowledgement |
| GST | Books to outward supplies, input-tax records, returns and portal ledgers | Resolved differences, approved return and payment receipts |
| Income tax | Financial records to income, deductions and available tax credits | Approved computation, filed return and verification status |
| Tax audit | Prescribed disclosures to books and supporting tests | Reviewed schedules, authorised report and portal receipt |

Obtain reviewer and client approval before submission. Keep the final return, computation, payment evidence and acknowledgement together. A draft or upload alone does not complete the engagement.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Tax-review checks</summary>

- **TDS:** Check payee details, payment category, applicable deduction, challan allocation, validation errors and credit/default mismatches. Track corrections separately.
- **GST:** Explain turnover and input-tax differences. Review credit/debit notes, reverse charge, eligibility, reversals, place of supply and carry-forward items before filing.
- **Income tax:** Check taxpayer details, income heads, losses, deductions, tax regime and credits. Resolve differences with portal information and confirm filing verification.
- **Tax audit:** Maintain a disclosure tracker. Check expenditure, depreciation, cash transactions, loans, vendor classifications and payment timing against the applicable requirements.
- Escalate disputed treatment, non-resident transactions, missing evidence and material defaults to the partner.

</details>

## Carry out the audit

Set the objective and risk first, then select the test and evidence. Keep the audit programme specific to the entity and engagement.

**Statutory audit:** Document materiality and risks; test significant balances, transactions and disclosures; evaluate misstatements, estimates, going concern and subsequent events. Obtain representations and partner approval before issuing the report.

**Bank concurrent audit:** Follow the bank-approved scope. Test cash, advances, security, KYC controls, remittances and unreconciled accounts. Escalate serious exceptions promptly and validate closure evidence.

**Internal audit:** Walk through the process, map risks to controls, test samples and agree findings with management. Each finding needs a business risk, action owner, due date and follow-up test.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Audit review and exception checklist</summary>

- Preserve the population, sample basis and transaction references.
- Link each conclusion to the work performed and the evidence retained.
- For statutory work, assess the applicable reporting requirements and annexures independently; clear significant judgments with the signing partner.
- For concurrent work, carry forward unresolved observations and verify rectification before closure.
- For internal audit, cover the agreed purchasing, sales, treasury, inventory, payroll and access controls. Keep management disagreements visible.
- Discuss report wording, scope limitations and uncorrected differences with the partner before release.

</details>

## Review, deliver and close

1. Clear preparer queries, reviewer notes and outstanding client items. Record unresolved matters and their treatment.
2. Obtain the required approvals, representations and signatures. Use authorised access for filing and verification.
3. Deliver the final accounts, report or return with a short completion note. State any remaining action and its deadline.
4. Archive the final version, approvals, receipts and correspondence. Restrict access and retain the file under the firm’s retention policy.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Workpaper and follow-up templates</summary>

**Workpaper:** Entity and period; objective; records tested; population and sample; work performed; result; conclusion; preparer; reviewer; date.

**Exception tracker:** Reference; issue; amount or exposure; evidence; severity; client response; action owner; due date; review status; closure evidence.

**Filing tracker:** Entity; return/report; period; reviewed amount; client approval; submission date; acknowledgement; verification status; next action.

**Weekly review:** Upcoming deadlines, missing records, high-risk findings, unresolved reconciliations and overdue client responses.

</details>
