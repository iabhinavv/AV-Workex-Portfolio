## 1. Scope, evidence and source boundary

This SOP uses the supplied `CAfirmSOPs.zip` (34 substantive files) and follows the structure of the supplied `GwoSevoSOPs` reference. It covers the work that a CA-firm team can perform using those formats, plus necessary control procedures. The archive contains 2023–2024 examples, not a complete current-law compliance manual.

Throughout the SOP, three labels keep the record accurate:

- **Source-backed [S]:** A named format, workbook, or guidance document appears in the supplied ZIP. Presence of a format does not prove that a client engagement was completed.
- **Replication standard [P]:** A recommended working step, checklist, review gate, or blank template added to make the practice repeatable. It is not evidence that the original firm used the step.
- **Current-law reference [R]:** Verified guidance on the relevant official portal, checked as of 23 November 2026. Applicability, dates, forms, amendments and exemptions must be rechecked for each client and filing period.

**Coverage distinction.** The ZIP directly supports statutory and tax-audit engagements, GST reconciliation, income-tax representations, MSME/43B(h) work, bookkeeping and financial schedules, and audit-trail reporting. It does **not** include a dedicated TDS statement template, a bank concurrent-audit program, or an internal-audit program. Those procedures and checklists below are new standards [P], informed by official guidance [R].

**Transition alert [R].** The Income-tax Act, 2025 and Income-tax Rules, 2026 apply from 1 April 2026. For FY 2025–26 / AY 2026–27, the department says the old Act and tax-audit Forms 3CA/3CB/3CD continue to apply. For Tax Year 2026–27, the prescribed tax-audit filing is consolidated Form 26. The historic references in the supplied documents must therefore be reassessed before reuse. See the official Income Tax forms FAQ in Section 20.

**Segregation of duties [P]:** A return/report is not filed merely because the preparer has completed the worksheet. The reviewer signs off on the draft; the client approves amounts and disclosures; only the authorised user completes the portal action. Never share OTPs, passwords or DSC keys in a workpaper or SOP.

## 4. Universal client file and review controls

**Input:** Engagement mandate, client identity, agreed period, records and statutory profiles.  
**Owner:** Engagement manager.  
**Output:** Controlled file with an audit trail from source to final submission.

1. Create a unique engagement ID; confirm legal name, PAN/TAN/GSTIN/CIN where applicable, period, scope, owner and partner.
2. Accept the engagement only after independence/conflict, competence, statutory eligibility, appointment and written terms are cleared; use the relevant archived engagement-letter format [S].
3. Maintain a deliverable calendar per entity and period. Confirm deadlines from official portals, notifications and extensions; do not embed last year's dates as fixed constants.
4. Issue a written records request. Maintain the **PBC register**: item, period, requested date, received date, source, status, deficiency and owner.
5. Put every working paper under `/ClientID/Period/Engagement/01_PBC/02_Source/03_Workpapers/04_Review/05_Final/06_Receipts`. Restrict access to the assigned team.
6. Each test must show **objective → source population → selection → work performed → finding → conclusion → preparer/date → reviewer/date**.
7. Keep a separate exceptions log. Do not overwrite original data or obscure a difference by plugging the balancing figure.
8. Use a four-state workflow: `Not started → Prepared → Reviewed → Filed / Report issued`; add `Blocked` and the reason when evidence is missing.
9. Record the exact portal submission acknowledgement and a copy of the final return/report, not just a screenshot of a draft.
10. Close only after receipts, client communication, pending issues and file archiving are complete. Set retention in line with applicable professional/legal requirements rather than assuming one universal period.

**Acceptance gate:** The file can be re-performed by a reviewer without asking the original preparer where the source data or final acknowledgement sits.

## 5. SOP: Onboard and scope a client engagement

**Basis:** Engagement letters, DSC authorisation and document handover certificate [S]; risk and acceptance steps [P].  
**Input:** Client request, entity data and intended service.  
**Output:** Approved engagement plan.

1. Determine whether the assignment is bookkeeping, periodic return filing, tax audit, statutory audit, bank concurrent audit or internal audit. Draft distinct scopes where the work differs.
2. Confirm statutory appointment or engagement authority, applicable standards, auditor eligibility and independence safeguards; identify services that may create conflict.
3. Obtain the signed engagement letter; specify management responsibilities, records access, deliverables, fees, timelines and limitations.
4. Request constitutional documents, previous-year reports/returns, financial statements, bank details, registrations, policies and prior observations.
5. Confirm who will approve filings and sign reports; authenticate portal access under the client's authorisation using approved secure procedures.
6. Prepare a deliverables calendar and a risk-ranked audit/program plan; assign preparer, reviewer and partner.
7. If information is incomplete, record outstanding requirements and hold the related filing/reporting step until reviewed.

**Source handoff:** `Engagement Letter for Audit of Companies.docx`, `Engagement Letter Tax Audit.docx`, `DSC Authorisation Certificate.doc` (adapt to current authorisation requirements).

## 6. SOP: Finalise books and build financial statements

**Basis:** `00.copy of BS PL FORMAT.xlsx`, general balance-sheet, income-statement and cash-flow templates [S]; review methodology [P].

1. Obtain opening balances, closing trial balance, ledger dump, general journal, bank statements, cashbook, fixed-asset register, debtor/creditor ageing and prior-year final figures.
2. Verify opening balances against prior signed financial statements and record unresolved movements.
3. Reconcile cash and all bank accounts; investigate stale cheques, old reconciling items, unrecorded fees and journal entries.
4. Check sales/purchases to statutory reports, credit/debit notes, inventory, debtors and creditors; test period-end cut-off.
5. Verify loans, borrowings, interest, related parties, payroll, statutory dues, depreciation, provisions and contingencies.
6. Prepare an adjusted trial balance and a separate journal adjustment register with reason, reference and approval.
7. Map balances to the applicable reporting framework; prepare balance sheet, statement of profit and loss, cash flows if required, notes and schedules.
8. Cross-foot statements to the signed trial balance; check comparative balances, accounting policies, rounding, related-party disclosures and appropriate signatories.
9. Have reviewer clear reconciliation exceptions and partner approve accounts/reporting decisions.

**Acceptance:** Every material financial-statement caption has a supporting lead schedule and documented review.

## 7. SOP: TDS computation, payment and quarterly statements

**Basis:** New replication procedure [P]; Income Tax Department manuals [R]. **No dedicated TDS template is in the ZIP.**

**Input:** Payment and payroll ledgers, vendor master, TAN/PAN, previous returns, challans, certificates and applicable tax-year rules.  
**Output:** Approved TDS register, tax payments, filed statement and reconciliation.

1. Create a period-specific TDS register: voucher/date, payee PAN, nature of payment, residential status, gross amount, relevant provision, threshold, applicable rate, deduction amount, deduction date, deposit date, challan and exception.
2. Reconcile the register to expense ledgers, contractor/professional charges, rent, interest, commission, salary and other potentially relevant accounts. Examine capitalised payments too.
3. Check triggering events, aggregate thresholds, lower/nil deduction certificates, PAN validity and special cases under the law applicable to **the relevant tax year and payment**.
4. Compare tax actually deducted and payable to ledger balances; investigate short deduction, late deduction, reversal and missing PAN.
5. Generate the correct tax-year challan, check the statute selected in the e-filing payment workflow, obtain client approval, and pay through an authorised channel. Download the challan/CIN.
6. Match each deductee transaction to its challan and reconcile totals by section, period and deductee before preparing the statement.
7. Determine the **correct form for the period**. The Income Tax Department identifies Form 138 (formerly 24Q) for salary/pension and Form 140 (formerly 26Q) for resident non-salary TDS under the 2025 Act. Form 141 consolidates specified PAN-based challan-cum-statements; other categories, including non-resident payments, require separate applicability checks [R].
8. Validate through the applicable portal/utility. Resolve file-validation errors, duplicate entries, invalid PAN, challan mismatches and short-payment flags before filing.
9. Obtain client and tax-reviewer approval of the statement summary. Submit; save filing acknowledgement, statement file, challans and correction-file history.
10. Reconcile portal processing, defaults/justification reports and deductee credit; issue applicable certificates and file corrections where approved.

**Review gates:** (a) legislation/form verified; (b) ledger-to-statement match; (c) challans match; (d) no unresolved validation errors; (e) acknowledgement saved.  
**Escalate:** Non-resident remittances, treaty positions, rate disputes, large defaults, missing records and form/date-transition ambiguity to the partner.

## 8. SOP: GST monthly, quarterly and year-end compliance

**Basis:** `GST Reconciliation Sheet.xlsx` [S], official GST portal manuals [R], additional review controls [P].

**Input:** Sales/purchase ledgers, invoice register, debit/credit notes, e-invoices, e-way bill records when applicable, bank receipts, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-2B, cash/credit ledgers.  
**Output:** Approved GST workings, filed returns and period/year-end variance log.

1. Confirm GSTIN, period, filing frequency/scheme, registration status and current notifications. Save data extracts with download timestamps.
2. Populate the archived workbook's **SALE FIGUERS** tab by month: books vs GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B, taxable turnover, exports and IGST/CGST/SGST; resolve each variance.
3. Populate **CREDIT NOTE** and **DEBIT NOTE** tabs; test invoice references, reason, timing and tax effect.
4. Populate **PURCHASE FIGUERS**: reconcile book-side input tax with GSTR-2B and GSTR-3B; classify unmatched, ineligible, blocked, reversed and deferred credits by evidence and law.
5. Populate **RCM**; verify applicable inward and outward reverse-charge classifications, tax payment and credit eligibility.
6. Populate **TAX DEPOSITED DETAIL** with CPIN/CIN, payment dates, tax head, interest and late fees; verify portal electronic cash and credit ledgers.
7. Reconcile the workbook's **SUMMARY** to books, prior-period balances and filed returns. Rebuild the **RATE WISE BREAKUP** from transaction-level evidence using **rates valid for each supply date**; do not assume the 2023–24 rate rows remain comprehensive.
8. Prepare GSTR-1/IFF where applicable from the outward-supply schedule. Review B2B/B2C classification, place of supply, amendments, exports, HSN summary and document count before filing.
9. Prepare GSTR-3B using verified liability, ITC, reversals, RCM, tax deposits and final ledgers. GST portal guidance states that GSTR-3B generally cannot be amended after filing; do not treat draft return adjustments as permissible post-filing corrections.
10. Obtain client approval, submit through authorised access, download both filed returns and payment receipts; update difference register and carry-forward items.
11. At year-end, assess GSTR-9 / GSTR-9C applicability, books-to-returns bridge, reversals, missed invoices, reclassifications and annual exposure; only file when applicable and independently reviewed.

**Acceptance:** All differences have a reason, owner and disposition (corrected / future adjustment / disclosure / unresolved); final filed return equals the reviewed schedule.

## 9. SOP: Income-tax return preparation and filing

**Basis:** `Representation Letter for ITR.docx`, DSC authorisation [S]; computation and submission controls [P]; official tax-year FAQ [R].

1. Confirm PAN, taxpayer category, residential status, FY/AY or Tax Year, prior-return position, audit applicability, due date and **governing Act**.
2. Obtain salary certificates, interest/investment statements, capital-gains data, property/rent details, business financials, deductions, brought-forward losses, advance-tax/TDS challans and foreign income/assets information where relevant.
3. Compare client records with Form 26AS, AIS/TIS and available portal disclosures; mark each difference with a decision and evidence.
4. Reconcile income to financial statements, GST turnover, TDS certificates, Form 16/16A and prior-year carried-forward attributes as applicable.
5. Prepare a computation with distinct heads of income, admissible deductions, tax regime, relief, set-off, surcharge/cess and tax credits under the applicable year’s statute; record assumptions and judgments.
6. Select the appropriate current-year ITR form and utility. Do not assume FY 2023–24 and Tax Year 2026–27 have identical forms or provisions.
7. Prepare the draft return and supporting schedule; reviewer independently checks PAN, form, residential status, banks/refund account, taxable income, tax credit and demand/refund.
8. Provide the computation/draft return to the client and obtain documented approval. Use the ITR representation format as a starting point, but update its old AY, statutory references and security language.
9. File through authorised access and approved verification method. Verify successful submission **and** completion of applicable e-verification/DSC steps.
10. Download acknowledgement, complete return, computation and verification status; send a clear client completion note and monitor processing/intimations separately.

**Acceptance:** Signed approval and verifiable filing/e-verification status. An upload without successful verification is not marked complete.

## 10. SOP: Tax audit and tax-audit reporting

**Basis:** Tax-audit engagement letter; Clause 44 workbook; 40A(3), 269SS/269T and MSME formats [S].

1. Decide applicability from current provisions and facts; confirm what financial reporting audit, if any, has been performed and by whom.
2. Obtain tax-audit engagement letter, final books, statutory returns, fixed-asset/depreciation register, bank loans, cashbook, vendor/MSME list and relevant agreements.
3. Map all tax-audit disclosure clauses to a **clause tracker** showing source, procedure, preparer, finding, reviewer and status; use the form prescribed for the specific period.
4. Verify turnover, accounting method, capital assets, depreciation, inadmissible expenditure, payments/cash restrictions, loans/deposits and reconciliation between P&L and return/computation.
5. Use the archived `Clause 44 Format.xlsx` as a legacy expenditure/GST reconciliation starting point **only where the reporting requirement remains applicable**; independently check the current form's structure and disclosures.
6. Request and verify MSME/Udyam status, supplier category, invoice dates, acceptance terms, due dates, payments and aged balances. Use `MSME Declaration.docx` and request letter as editable examples, not as conclusive evidence without corroboration.
7. For source-era 43B(h) matters, document the chosen testing basis, completeness limits and conclusion; don't paste any of the archived sample disclaimer scenarios without partner review and a current-law mapping.
8. Test 40A(3) and 269SS/269T issues against ledger, bank/cash entries and applicable exceptions. Management certificates are supplementary representations, **not substitutes for audit evidence**.
9. Draft the prescribed audit form; for FY 2025–26 / AY 2026–27 the official department states Forms 3CA/3CB/3CD remain applicable; for Tax Year 2026–27 it identifies consolidated **Form 26** [R].
10. Resolve partner review notes and report modifications; obtain client confirmation, manage the prescribed portal workflow, and retain final report, UDIN where applicable and acknowledgement.

**Acceptance:** Tax-audit disclosure schedule reconciles to books and supporting workpapers; legally required report version is correct for the period.

## 11. SOP: Statutory financial-statement audit

**Basis:** `Engagement Letter for Audit of Companies.docx`, `MRL.doc`, statutory report with CARO annexure, non-corporate report, Rule 11(g) notes [S]; SA-based methodology [P/R].

**Stage A — Plan and assess risk**

1. Verify auditor appointment, eligibility and independence, applicable Act/framework, component structure, prior-year observations and engagement terms.
2. Understand the entity, industry, IT systems, financial reporting process and controls. Set materiality/performance materiality and identify significant risks with documented rationale.
3. Prepare audit strategy, team allocation, sampling plan, PBC list and response to assessed risks.

**Stage B — Obtain evidence**

4. Perform control walkthroughs and test controls where reliance is planned; substantively test revenue, purchases, payroll, cash/bank, receivables, inventory, fixed assets, debt, related parties, statutory dues and estimates.
5. Obtain external confirmations where appropriate; test journals, unusual transactions, period-end cut-off and management override; corroborate responses with underlying evidence.
6. Check financial-statement disclosures, subsequent events, going concern, commitments/contingencies, directors' representations and significant subsequent adjustments.
7. For company audits, test accounting software inventory, edit-log enablement, coverage, changes, access and preservation for **Rule 11(g)** reporting, guided by the archived 2024 notes and current ICAI guidance.
8. Assess internal financial controls reporting and **CARO applicability** separately; do not reuse the archived 2024 report's entity-specific exemptions, thresholds or assertions without new analysis.

**Stage C — Conclude and report**

9. Aggregate misstatements; assess uncorrected items, disclosure gaps and scope limitations; evaluate impact on the opinion and other reporting responsibilities.
10. Obtain tailored management representation letter near the report date; obtain governance communication and sufficient final review evidence.
11. Draft the applicable audit report and annexures; independently verify every assertion, entity data, dates, wording, legal reporting clauses, UDIN and signing requirements.
12. Issue the signed report only after partner approval; archive final statements, evidence, MRL, closing checklist and delivery correspondence.

**Acceptance:** Risk-responsive workpapers, documented partner judgments and a report appropriate to the specific entity; no unsupported boilerplate.

## 12. SOP: Bank concurrent audit

**Basis:** **New** audit program [P]. The ZIP has no bank concurrent-audit workpaper. The RBI's **Commercial Banks – Concurrent Audit Directions, 2026**, updated 1 October 2026, provide the current official frame for commercial banks [R]; other bank categories require their own applicable directions.

**Purpose:** Timely transaction examination and early detection of control weaknesses.  
**Frequency:** Per the bank-approved engagement scope and reporting calendar, commonly continuous/periodic with monthly reporting where mandated by the bank.  
**Output:** Branch/unit exception log, monthly report and closure tracker.

1. Obtain the letter of appointment, **bank-approved scope**, covered units/processes, materiality/reporting thresholds, data access, sample rules and escalation contacts.
2. Document independence, staff authorisation, access controls and confidentiality. Review prior reports, unresolved high-risk findings and bank/RBI circulars in force for the period.
3. Select and record risk-based/random samples from transaction populations; preserve system-generated populations and sample parameters.
4. Check cash and physical cash verification; large cash and unusual cash transactions; vault and dual-control procedures.
5. Check loan sanction and delegation, disbursement, security creation/registration, end-use, drawing powers, overdue monitoring, classification, security inspection and exceptions to terms.
6. Check KYC/AML, risk classification, account opening, staff accounts, transaction monitoring and compliance reporting against the bank's control procedures.
7. Check remittances, clearing, RTGS/NEFT/SWIFT where relevant, bills, foreign exchange and other branch-specific activities.
8. Reconcile suspense, sundry and parking accounts, GL/SGL and inter-branch items; review unreconciled/old entries and manual overrides.
9. Cover non-fund-based business, cards, merchant banking, treasury, employee conduct and mis-selling wherever in the approved scope and applicable to the unit.
10. For each exception, capture reference number, condition, policy breached, amount/exposure, cause, severity, branch response, recommended action and evidence. **Immediately escalate serious fraud red flags, control failure or regulator-reportable items through the mandated route.**
11. Issue the concurrent audit report to bank-designated recipients, track replies and evidence of closure; carry forward aged observations and do not mark 'rectified' without validation.

**Acceptance:** Population/sample traceability; timely escalation; report follows the bank's approved format, not an invented universal RBI report template.

## 13. SOP: Internal audit

**Basis:** **New** audit program [P]; source financial/reconciliation schedules can be reused as evidence [S]. Companies Act section 138 establishes internal-audit requirements for prescribed classes of companies [R]. For banks, use the applicable 2026 RBI internal-audit directions instead of a generic corporate program.

**Input:** Internal audit charter/engagement terms, audit universe, business process maps, policies, financial MIS, control register, prior findings.  
**Output:** Risk-based internal audit report and action plan.

1. Agree audit objectives, scope, independence, reporting line, location, period, materiality/risk thresholds and access rights.
2. Build audit universe and risk assessment: revenue, procurement, inventory, payroll, treasury, statutory compliance, IT access, financial close, related parties and fraud vulnerabilities.
3. Build a **risk-control matrix (RCM)**: risk, control objective, owner, process description, expected evidence, frequency, inherent risk and planned testing.
4. Conduct walkthroughs and identify design gaps; select samples for operating-effectiveness testing and document the basis.
5. Test purchase-to-pay: vendor onboarding, quotations, purchase orders, 3-way match, approvals, duplicate payments, MSME and TDS/GST controls.
6. Test order-to-cash: customer creation, pricing, invoicing, revenue cut-off, credit limits, receivables, collections and sales-return authority.
7. Test cash/bank/treasury: bank mandates, reconciliations, cash custody, payments, maker-checker controls and dormant account monitoring.
8. Test inventory/physical assets: receipt/issue controls, stock counts, movement records, obsolescence, disposals and fixed-asset verification.
9. Test payroll and HR, IT access/change logs, backups, outsourced service providers and incident escalation.
10. Test filing completeness and book-to-return reconciliation for tax/GST/TDS as **control objectives**, without presenting the work as a separate statutory certification.
11. Rate findings against a documented impact/likelihood rubric; agree owners, target dates and action evidence. Disclose management disagreement distinctly.
12. Issue a draft for factual validation, then a final report to the agreed oversight body; retest remediation on the agreed schedule.

**Acceptance:** Every observation links to a test/evidence, states a business risk, and has an accountable action owner and due date.

## 18. Quality-assurance and replication sequence

**Phase 1 — Set up:** Create client and engagement registers; convert the archive into approved firm-controlled templates; flag obsolete dates/statutory references in each original.

**Phase 2 — Pilot:** Run one representative engagement per relevant workstream with two levels of review; record recurring defects, unclear templates and portal errors.

**Phase 3 — Standardise:** Assign workpaper IDs and required evidence fields; control report versions; provide training on GST reconciliation, TDS/ITR checks, audit trail and audit sampling.

**Phase 4 — Monitor:** Review filing timeliness, portal defaults, unresolved variances, audit review-note closure, repeat observations and delayed PBC items. Use actual measured results only—no synthetic benchmarks.

**Release rule:** Each template is marked `Current and approved`, `Needs update`, or `Reference only`; source-year forms cannot automatically be issued to a current client.
